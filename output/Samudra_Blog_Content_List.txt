SAMUDRA ADJUSTING
PROPOSED BLOG AND INSIGHTS CONTENT PLAN

Prepared for client review | Schedule anchor: Day 1 = 5 October 2026

PURPOSE
This document extracts the 13 proposed blog articles from the 60-day content plan: seven in-depth guides and six shorter practical or market updates. Each article is designed to answer a real question from shipowners, charterers, brokers, claims handlers or seafarers, with practical explanations and links to reliable sources.

The dates below are based on a Day 1 start of 5 October 2026. The publishing programme is conditional on the website passing the plan's Day 10 crawlability and content-readiness review. If that review does not pass, the publishing schedule should be paused and reset before articles go live.

All legal, regulatory, market, service and professional-credential statements will be checked against appropriate sources and confirmed with Samudra before publication. The outlines below describe proposed coverage, not final approved copy. No article is legal, insurance or medical advice.

IN-DEPTH GUIDES

1. WHAT AN AVERAGE ADJUSTER ACTUALLY DOES IN A MARINE INSURANCE CLAIM
Planned publication: Day 11 | 15 October 2026

Purpose and readers
An introductory guide for shipowners, charterers, cargo interests, brokers and claims handlers who want to understand an average adjuster's role and what to expect when a marine claim is handled.

Proposed coverage
- Explain the role of an average adjuster in plain language and distinguish the adjuster's role from that of an insurer, lawyer, surveyor or P&I club.
- Walk through the claim process: notification; instruction and survey; evidence gathering; cover review; loss assessment; reporting and settlement; and potential recovery.
- Describe the kinds of documents a claimant may be asked to provide, after checking the list against Samudra's actual intake process.
- Explain the difference between an independent appointment and an appointment made for a particular party, without disparaging any party's appointed adjuster.
- State clearly what Samudra does and does not do, using only client-approved service and credential information.

2. GENERAL AVERAGE VS PARTICULAR AVERAGE: A SINGAPORE SHIPOWNER'S GUIDE
Planned publication: Day 16 | 20 October 2026

Purpose and readers
A practical explanation for shipowners, cargo interests, brokers and claims handlers trying to understand how a marine loss may be treated and what can happen after a general average declaration.

Proposed coverage
- Define general average and particular average, and explain the distinction between a shared sacrifice and a loss borne by one interest.
- Use clear illustrative scenarios to show how the distinction works, including an edge case where an intentional act does not preserve the common maritime adventure.
- Outline the typical steps following a general average declaration, such as preparation of a statement, provision of security, assessment of contributions and subsequent recovery steps.
- List the core information and documents a reader may need to gather.
- Include Singapore-specific statutory material only after checking the current primary legislation and confirming the wording with the client. Present the article as general information, not legal advice.

3. HULL AND MACHINERY CLAIMS: THE SEVEN STAGES, END TO END
Planned publication: Day 23 | 27 October 2026

Purpose and readers
A step-by-step guide for shipowners, operators, brokers and claims teams managing physical damage to a vessel.

Proposed coverage
- Explain what a hull and machinery claim concerns and direct readers to their own policy for the scope of cover.
- Set out seven stages: notification; appointment and survey; protecting the vessel and preventing further damage; damage assessment; causation and quantum; settlement; and recovery where relevant.
- Explain the documents and evidence commonly involved, subject to confirmation against Samudra's actual claims intake requirements.
- Describe what a useful survey report contains, including the distinction between observed findings and conclusions or matters that remain uncertain.
- Discuss common sources of delay as qualitative observations only. Do not include firm-specific statistics, turnaround promises or recovery figures unless verified and approved.

4. P&I LETTER OF UNDERWRITING IN SINGAPORE: WHAT IT IS AND WHEN YOU NEED ONE
Planned publication: Day 30 | 3 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A plain-language guide for shipowners, charterers, brokers and claims handlers who encounter a P&I letter of undertaking (LOU) when security is requested.

Proposed coverage
- Explain what an LOU is intended to do and how it differs from providing cash security.
- Describe the kinds of information an LOU may address, without reproducing or offering template wording for a bespoke instrument.
- Explain situations in which security may be requested and identify the questions a reader should raise with their lawyer, broker or club.
- Make clear that an LOU is not, by itself, an admission that a claim is valid or successful.
- Confirm the exact title and terminology with the Founder before drafting: the plan's working title says "Letter of Underwriting," while the brief describes a "Letter of Undertaking." Verify the relevant Singapore security discussion against primary sources and obtain appropriate review before publication.

5. THIRD-PARTY COLLISION LIABILITY: HOW FAULT GETS APPORTIONED
Planned publication: Day 37 | 10 November 2026

Purpose and readers
An evidence-led introduction for shipowners, operators, brokers and claims handlers dealing with a collision involving two vessels.

Proposed coverage
- Explain, in general terms, how damage and responsibility may be considered between vessels, and why the outcome is not always a simple matter of each side paying half.
- Describe the evidence that can inform an assessment, such as collision regulations, voyage data, AIS information, witness accounts, damage patterns and expert reconstruction.
- Explain the role a collision clause may play in recovery without reproducing policy wording.
- Cover the practical questions that arise when the other vessel is unidentified or cannot readily be pursued, only after the relevant provisions are checked.
- Include Singapore-specific legal or treaty framing only after the applicable primary source is identified and verified. Avoid unverified percentages, statutory references and predictions.

6. CREW ILLNESS AND REPATRIATION CLAIMS: WHAT THE CLUB ACTUALLY PAYS
Planned publication: Day 44 | 17 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A clear, considerate guide for shipowners, crew, families, brokers and claims handlers navigating a crew illness or injury matter and related P&I cover questions.

Proposed coverage
- Explain, at a high level, how P&I cover may respond to crew illness, injury and repatriation, while emphasizing that the applicable club rules and entry terms control.
- Describe the kinds of medical and employment information that may be relevant to a claim, without making a medical assessment or prognosis.
- Explain what repatriation may involve and how the destination is determined, only after checking the applicable wording and obtaining client confirmation.
- Offer a practical list of information a crew member, family or operator may wish to keep and whom they may need to contact.
- Distinguish the adjuster's role in assessing a claim from medical care, treatment decisions and fitness-for-duty decisions. Do not reproduce policy wording or state unverified entitlements as universal rules.

7. CHOOSING AN INDEPENDENT MARINE CLAIMS ADJUSTER IN SINGAPORE: 9 QUESTIONS
Planned publication: Day 51 | 24 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A buyer-focused guide for shipowners, brokers, claims managers and other parties comparing adjusters before making an appointment.

Proposed coverage
- Present nine practical questions: who will do the work; what claim types they handle and how recently; independence and conflicts; geographic reach; professional memberships; conflict procedures; report access; fees and payment; and circumstances in which the firm would decline an appointment.
- Explain what useful, transparent answers look like, including the documents a client may wish to review before appointing an adjuster.
- Include Samudra's own answers only after the Founder confirms them, including the named practitioner, current credentials, service scope, independence position, geographic coverage and fee approach.
- Keep the comparison fair and useful. Do not claim unverified experience, memberships, locations, case outcomes or advantages over named competitors.

SHORTER PRACTICAL AND MARKET ARTICLES

8. SINGAPORE'S MARINE INSURANCE MARKET IN 2026: THE RECORD YEAR, IN NUMBERS
Planned publication: Day 18 | 22 October 2026

Purpose and readers
A sourced snapshot for shipowners, brokers and claims professionals who want context on Singapore's maritime and marine insurance environment.

Proposed coverage
- Summarize selected 2025 indicators from the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore's Annual Report, including container throughput, vessel arrivals, Singapore Registry of Ships tonnage and maritime-sector business spending.
- Explain the scale of Singapore's marine insurance and broking market using the source's own qualified wording.
- Link readers directly to the original MPA sources and distinguish reported facts from commentary.
- If bunker sales are mentioned, state the fuel scope and use the Annual Report figure consistently; otherwise omit the figure. Do not mix revised annual-report data with earlier provisional trade-press figures.
- Avoid forecasts or unsupported conclusions about future claim volumes or market conditions.

9. THE INTERNATIONAL GROUP P&I 2026/27 RENEWAL: WHAT CHANGED
Planned publication: Day 25 | 29 October 2026

Purpose and readers
A neutral explainer for shipowners, charterers, brokers and claims professionals following the International Group's published 2026/27 reinsurance arrangements.

Proposed coverage
- Summarize the published reinsurance structure and the applicable policy-year dates.
- Present the published rate changes by vessel category in a clearly labelled table, with figures checked directly against International Group material.
- Explain what the published changes may mean in general terms for owners and charterers, without treating them as a forecast of an individual vessel's premium or renewal terms.
- Clarify the distinction between reinsurance arrangements and the handling of an individual claim.
- Link to primary sources and a relevant club update. Re-check the article before the end of the plan because renewal information can change.

10. WRECK REMOVAL IN SINGAPORE: WHEN THE DIRECTOR OF MARINE STEPS IN
Planned publication: Day 32 | 5 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A concise Singapore-focused guide for vessel owners, operators, brokers and claims handlers seeking to understand the authority's potential role after a wreck.

Proposed coverage
- Explain, with careful attribution, the circumstances in which the Director of Marine may require a registered owner to remove a wreck that constitutes a hazard.
- Distinguish the authority's role from questions of insurance response, liability and who ultimately bears costs.
- Describe practical considerations for owners and claims teams after a casualty, without presenting an interpretive description of "hazard" as a statutory test.
- Link to the applicable primary legal or government source and have the legal framing checked before publication.
- Make clear that Samudra is not the authority or the insurer and that the piece is general information, not legal advice.

11. SIRE AND OCIMF: WHY VESSEL INSPECTION STANDARDS FOLLOW YOUR INSURANCE
Planned publication: Day 39 | 12 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A technical overview for shipowners, operators, brokers and vetting professionals interested in how vessel inspection information may be considered in vetting and insurance discussions.

Proposed coverage
- Explain the SIRE inspection context and the role of OCIMF using OCIMF's own published material.
- Discuss how inspection reports may inform vetting and related commercial conversations without claiming that an inspection guarantees insurance terms or prevents a casualty.
- Emphasize that an inspection is a record of findings at a particular time, not a permanent rating of a vessel.
- Publication in this form depends on written confirmation of the relevant practitioner credential and confirmation of the technical claims. If that credential is not confirmed, use the planned alternative topic: "What a Vetting Report Is and Why Your Insurer Reads It," without implying Samudra has a credential or endorsement it cannot substantiate.

12. HOW TO READ THE CLAIMS NOTIFICATION CLAUSE IN YOUR MARINE POLICY
Planned publication: Day 46 | 19 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A practical reading guide for shipowners, operators, brokers and claims teams reviewing notification provisions in a marine policy.

Proposed coverage
- Explain that policy wordings may contain distinct notification obligations and that the wording itself determines what must be notified and when.
- Show readers how to locate the relevant clause, identify what event starts a period and distinguish an initial notification from fuller claim details.
- Provide a short checklist of questions to raise with their broker, insurer or lawyer.
- Use the Founder's actual policy wordings as the source before describing any clause structure. Do not invent or generalize deadlines, reproduce policy wording, or state that late notice automatically forfeits cover.
- Keep the piece educational, not legal advice or a promise to review a policy.

13. PDPA AND GDPR IN MARINE CLAIMS HANDLING: A SINGAPORE PRACTICAL GUIDE
Planned publication: Day 53 | 26 November 2026

Purpose and readers
A practice-focused overview for shipowners, operators, crew, families and claims professionals considering the personal information that may be handled during a marine claim.

Proposed coverage
- Describe the kinds of information that may appear in a claims file, such as identity, employment, medical, correspondence and financial records.
- Explain the firm's own documented practices for access, retention, security, individual requests and cross-border handling, only where those practices have been confirmed and are actually in place.
- Base any Singapore privacy-law statements on current PDPC guidance and primary legal sources. Have the treatment of EU-related questions reviewed; do not offer an unsupported opinion about when the GDPR applies.
- Confirm that the website's privacy notice and terms are live, accurate and linked before publishing a description of the firm's privacy practices.
- State plainly that the article describes the firm's practice and is not legal advice or a compliance certification.
