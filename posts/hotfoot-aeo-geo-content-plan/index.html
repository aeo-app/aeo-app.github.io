Wingzaway EMP — Full 30-Day AEO/GEO Schedule & Content Library (Singapore + Asia)

October 8, 2026

What this document is#

This is the working AEO/GEO plan for Wingzaway EMP, the bird deterrent product supplied and installed by Hot Foot Asia. It is built around five commercial-intent keywords in Singapore and broader Asia search, with a simple goal: make the brand easy to find, easy to trust, and easy to contact when a building owner or facilities team is searching for a way to stop pigeons from roosting on roofs, solar arrays, façades or warehouse zones.

This plan treats the website like a commercial search asset, not just a brochure. The strategy is built around actual buyer intent: people are not browsing casually; they are usually trying to solve an active bird problem, often with a pressing operational or safety issue.

The plan contains four things:

  1. Company and market research — what Wingzaway is, who it serves, where it is relevant, and what the product story should be in search.
  2. The keyword panel — the five target queries that define the campaign.
  3. The 30-day schedule — the execution plan for content, technical SEO, conversion and AI visibility.
  4. The content library — the key pages and supporting assets needed to own the cluster.

Company research: what Wingzaway is and what it sells#

Business positioning#

The company website positions Wingzaway as an electromagnetic pulse bird deterrent designed to stop pigeons from landing and roosting without drilling, spikes or netting. The core selling proposition is:

  • no drilling into roof or façade
  • no spikes or netting
  • no harm to birds
  • low-visibility, low-maintenance deterrence
  • suitable for rooftops, façades, solar arrays and industrial plant areas
  • supplied and installed across Asia by Hot Foot Asia

This is a clear product-led proposition. It is not a “general pest control” business. It is a commercial bird deterrent technology for property managers, facilities teams, solar operators and industrial operators.

What the website says the problem is#

The landing page frames the core problem clearly:

  • droppings corrode cladding, gutters, parapets and roof membranes
  • nests block drains and vents
  • rooftop solar arrays become nesting voids and reduce energy output
  • bird activity creates health exposure and audit risk in food, pharma and healthcare facilities
  • equipment can overheat or fail when birds nest near AC units, cowls and cable trays
  • temporary deterrents can become recurring maintenance costs

That is a strong AEO/GEO starting point because the brand is already solving a real operational problem with a clear commercial angle.

Target audience#

The strongest buyer types are:

  • Facilities managers
  • Property owners and building managers
  • Solar farm or rooftop array owners
  • Warehouse operators and logistics managers
  • Manufacturing and plant operators
  • Commercial real estate teams
  • Hospitals, food facilities, pharma and other audited environments

These buyers are often searching not for “bird control,” but for a specific issue: how to stop pigeons from landing on a rooftop, how to protect solar panels, or how to avoid visible netting on a building façade.

Geography and market fit#

The website is explicitly positioned as Asia-wide. For this plan, the strongest local priority is Singapore, because the target keywords are location-specific and the search intent is highly commercial. The Singapore market makes sense for the campaign because:

  • dense urban rooftops and façades create ideal roosting conditions
  • rooftop solar arrays are common in commercial and industrial properties
  • building owners look for non-invasive alternatives to netting and spikes
  • facilities teams value durability and maintenance reduction

The plan therefore uses Singapore as the market anchor and Asia as the broader, product-level expansion layer.

Brand narrative to use in content#

Wingzaway has a strong usable story:

  • birds are not just a nuisance; they create asset damage and operational risk
  • netting and spikes are visible and often impractical for rooftops and façades
  • a non-invasive deterrent can be more suitable for commercial sites
  • the business provides supply, installation and servicing across markets

This story is highly compatible with AEO/GEO because it answers the exact questions people ask when they already have a bird problem.

Why these five matter#

These are not generic bird-control keywords. They map to the decision journey of a commercial buyer:

  1. The site has a real problem.
  2. The buyer wants a solution that protects the building or equipment.
  3. The buyer wants a method that is not visually intrusive or operationally disruptive.
  4. The buyer wants a credible answer and likely a site assessment.

That is exactly the intent cluster an AEO/GEO strategy should own.

Strategic objective#

The goal is not just visibility. The goal is to make Wingzaway the answer when a property manager, facilities team or warehouse operator asks:

  • how do I stop pigeons landing on my building?
  • what is the best bird deterrent for a rooftop solar array?
  • is there a bird deterrent without netting or spikes?
  • what does warehouse bird control cost in Singapore?
  • what is an electromagnetic pulse pigeon control system?

The plan therefore aims to do three things at once:

  1. Capture local commercial searches in Singapore.
  2. Own the product/category language around EMP bird deterrent.
  3. Build trust with educational pages that explain the problem clearly before leading into a quote or assessment request.

Why this plan is shaped this way#

Why the schedule includes two blogs per full sprint week#

The 30-day sprint includes two distinct blog publications in each complete seven-day block (Days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21 and 22–28). Commercial service and product landing pages are separate deliverables and do not count toward this cadence. Days 29–30 are the closeout window, not a fifth full week; the Day 29 supporting article remains optional and does not replace measurement. Blog claims about product performance, safety, bird behaviour, installation, price, ROI or damage must be sourced and approved before publication; otherwise, omit them.

Why the campaign is built around a small, specific keyword cluster#

There are five core queries, but they sit inside a much larger category: bird deterrent, pigeon control, rooftop bird control, solar panel bird deterrent, warehouse bird control, and non-invasive deterrent systems. A small cluster is enough if each page is strong and well-linked. There is no need to overbuild a large generic pest-content library.

Why this is site- and conversion-led#

The Wingzaway site already has the right product story and a clear value proposition. The missing layer is structure: the pages need to be mapped to the actual questions buyers are asking, and the conversion path needs to be obvious.

Why Singapore is the anchor#

The Singapore keyword form is explicit commercial intent and easier to own because it is local and specific. Once the Singapore pages are working, they can be expanded into broader Asia pages with product language.

Why the pages should not read like a brochure alone#

Commercial buyers want proof and clarity. Each page should answer the problem, explain the solution in plain language, show where it works, and end with a practical CTA.

Who owns what#

Use the placeholder roles below as a working model.

  • Founder / commercial owner — approves all copy and claims, owns the final CTA and site-assessment workflow, decides which case examples or project language can be used.
  • Content / SEO lead — owns page briefs, keyword mapping, FAQs, on-page copy, pillar page structure, and internal linking.
  • Technical / GEO lead — owns schema, page indexing, AI visibility checks, Search Console, sitemap and crawlability.
  • Sales / operations — owns site assessment form flow, enquiry routing, quote eligibility and lead follow-up.

This is a simple ownership model because the business is small and the content cluster is clear.
