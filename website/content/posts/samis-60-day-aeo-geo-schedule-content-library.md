<a id="notes"></a>

## Notes

Decisions that are not visible in the table but change how the plan runs.

1. **Why the plan publishes nothing for the first 10 days.** Publishing onto a page that no crawler can read is the single most expensive mistake available here. A post published into a client-rendered React shell with a `create-react-app` placeholder description has effectively no chance of being indexed or cited, because there is no HTML for an engine to extract. The Foundation phase is therefore not "prep" — it is the deliverable. The Day 10 gate can and does block all 26 pieces of Phase 2 content.

2. **The 60-day window is positioned, not arbitrary.** The International Group P&I policy year runs **20 February to 20 February** (the 2026/27 year is 20 Feb 2026 – 20 Feb 2027, per the club renewal circular dated 18 February 2026). A 60-day plan starting 5 October 2026 ends in early December — roughly ten weeks before the **20 February 2027** renewal. That is the whole strategic point: shipowners, charterers and brokers choosing a claims adjuster in the Feb 2027 window will be reading whatever Samudra published in the preceding two quarters. Pillar P7 ("9 Questions to Ask Before You Appointing") and YouTube Y6 are written to be the thing a broker forwards internally before that appointment. **AEO content in this niche has a renewal cycle, not a news cycle.**

3. **Why LinkedIn and YouTube are first-class columns, not "also do social".** This is a sourced decision, not a preference. Meltwater's July 2026 GenAI Lens analysis ranks **YouTube as the single largest AI-citation source** (229.3K citations in July 2026, +24.3% month-on-month) and records **LinkedIn as the largest multi-metric gainer of any top-10 source** (+67.8% citation volume, +28.6% citation rate, moving from #8 in June to #5 in July at 80.3K citations). For a professional-services firm whose entire value is a named human's credentials, LinkedIn is not a broadcast channel — it is the **citable identity surface** that AI engines read when they assemble "who is this person". Founders in this trade post from a personal profile; see [Standing playbooks](#li-standing).

4. **Why the site is rebuilt rather than "SEO'd".** `samis.com.sg` is a `create-react-app` shell. There is no meta description that isn't the CRA default, no canonical, no schema, no OG card, no real sitemap, and no per-route HTML. Optimising what is there is not an option — there is nothing there to optimise. The minimum viable fix is server-rendered or prerendered HTML per route.

5. **Why the blog origin is replaced, not repaired.** The site's `/blogs` route fetches `https://wordpress.samis.com.sg/wp-json/wp/v2/posts`. That host resolves to `18.143.103.43` and **port 443 is not reachable** — verified by direct connection attempt and three consecutive HTTP timeouts on 29 Sep 2026. The whole "Technical Resources & Insights" surface is already dead in production. Content must land somewhere reachable; Day 7 decides and builds the replacement.

6. **The Deepavali blackout is real and it is in the table.** Deepavali 2026 falls on **Sunday 8 November**, so **Monday 9 November is a gazetted Singapore public holiday**. Those are Days 35 and 36. Nothing publishes, nothing is due, and no gate sits on them. The table shows them as such. (Christmas Day 25 December 2026 falls after Day 60, so it does not affect this plan.)

7. **Rest rhythm.** Saturdays and Sundays carry no publishable deliverable. They carry the two things that must happen off-hours: the AI answer-engine prompt panel (Content–SEO, Sunday) and the weekly tracker update. In a niche where the audience is adjusters, brokers, P&I club claims handlers and shipowners, a Saturday LinkedIn post from a brand with no established presence does more harm than good.

8. **Publishing day is set by the table, not by the rhythm note.** Tuesdays are pillars, Thursdays are tacticals, Wednesdays are YouTube, Tuesdays/Thursdays/Fridays are LinkedIn, Mondays are outbound. If a day is missed, it is **made up inside the same week** or dropped at the Midpoint Checkpoint — never silently moved.

## Decisions, assumptions and open questions

**Decisions taken in this plan (and why):**

| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Rebuild to SSR/SSG rather than optimise the SPA | No indexable HTML exists to optimise. Source: direct fetch of 8 routes, 29 Sep 2026. |
| Replace the WordPress blog origin rather than restore it | `18.143.103.43:443` unreachable. Restoring is an infra project outside a 60-day marketing window; the content survives in the ledger and migrates. |
| Build the blog into the same app as the marketing site | One origin, one sitemap, one crawl budget, one deploy. Removes the failure mode that created this problem. |
| Source Ledger as a hard gate | Marine claims copy carries legal and financial consequence. An unsourced figure in this niche is a liability, not a marketing error. |

## Source Ledger

**This is the gate. Nothing publishes that is not in this ledger with a live source URL and a verification status. No row → no claim.**

A marine claims firm that publishes a wrong figure about a P&I club, a bunker statistic or a legal provision does not lose a marketing campaign — it loses the professional standing the entire firm is built on, and the referring brokers who send work. This is the strictest rule in the plan.

**Ledger columns:** `Claim ID | Claim (exact wording to be published) | Source URL | Source owner | Publication date | Retrieved | Verified by | Status`

**Status values:** `VERIFIED` (fetched the source, the number is exactly as stated) · `VERIFIED-QUALIFIED` (true, but only publishable with the stated qualifier attached) · `UNVERIFIED` (do not publish) · `CONFLICT` (sources disagree — see C4) · `WITHDRAWN`.

**The ledger is created on Day 8 and is owned by Content–SEO, approved by the Founder. Every brief below that needs a figure names the Claim IDs it needs.**

### CONFLICT C4 — the Singapore bunker sales figure

Two figures for 2025 Singapore bunker sales are both in circulation and **they are not the same measurement**:

## Briefs & Prompts

### How to read every brief below

Every brief has the same five parts, so you never have to guess what one field means:

- **Topic** — what is being made, and which buyer question it is built to be the answer to.
- **Why** — one line of positioning, so the tone is not left to the writer's inference.
- **What to include, in order** — numbered and specific: section-by-section for a post, beat-by-beat for a video, step-by-step for an outreach session.
- **Ready-to-use copy** — a full draft you can paste. Anything needing a real fact is a `[SQUARE-BRACKET PLACEHOLDER]`, and **every placeholder must be filled with a real, Founder-approved, Ledger-backed value before publishing.** A placeholder that ships is a broken promise; a placeholder that becomes a guess is worse.
- **Checklist before publishing** — named checks. **If any box is unticked, do not publish.**

**Claim IDs are load-bearing.** Where a brief names `Claim ID`s, the writer must carry those IDs in the draft. If the writer cannot produce an ID for a claim, the claim does not ship.

### Brand rules

No stock photography of generic cargo ships, containers or sunsets. A claims firm's credibility is documents, reports, people and process. Use the real (anonymised) documents: a claim status timeline, a survey report cover, a security instrument, a process diagram built in brand colours.

### The Answer-First rule (applies to every blog, LinkedIn and YouTube asset)

An AI answer engine has to lift a self-contained answer out of your page and cite it. So:

1. **Answer in the first two lines.** Not a preamble. Not "in today's world". The answer.
2. **Then the keyword phrase, naturally, in a heading or the first sentence after the answer.**
3. **Then substance** — the thing a practitioner actually needs, in the order they need it.
4. **Then the sourced specifics**, each carrying its Claim ID, so a citation has something to attach to.
5. **Then a direct question** that invites the reader to ask for help, phrased as the buyer would phrase it.

This structure is not stylistic preference. Structured content is the documented lever: research on generative engine optimization found **up to 40% more visibility in generative-engine answers** when content is structured for it (`AEO-08`) — and structure is also the one thing the measurement says matters, since AI engines name B2B brands **23.9%** of the time but only **cite** them **16.5%** of the time (`AEO-02`). Getting named is cheap. Getting cited is the actual objective.

### AEO asset checklist — run on every blog post before it publishes

- [ ] First two lines answer the question outright
- [ ] Target keyword phrase present in the H1 or the first sentence after the answer
- [ ] Every number, legal provision, market figure and credential carries a Claim ID
- [ ] Every Claim ID is `VERIFIED` or `VERIFIED-QUALIFIED` — **not** `UNVERIFIED`, `CONFLICT` or `WITHDRAWN`
- [ ] Where a figure is `VERIFIED-QUALIFIED`, the qualifier is in the sentence, not in a footnote
- [ ] Any proxy statistic is labelled as a proxy in the same sentence (`AEO-03` is B2B **software**; `AEO-06`/`AEO-07` are vendor audits, attributed)
- [ ] No bunker figure published without its fuel scope (CONFLICT C4)
- [ ] No claim that the firm is a law firm, insurer, P&I club or class society
- [ ] No case study detail without a cleared `CRD-*` or A5 release
- [ ] H1 unique across the site; `<title>` ≤60 chars and contains the brand or the service
- [ ] Meta description 140–160 chars, answer-first, unique
- [ ] Canonical self-referencing and absolute
- [ ] JSON-LD `Article` present and matching the visible content; FAQ marked up **only** if the questions and answers are visibly on the page
- [ ] Internal links to at least two other Samudra pages, plus at least one outbound link to a Ledger source

### LinkedIn post template

**The Founder posts from a personal profile, not the company page.** This is not vanity — it is the whole point. In a professional-services trade the asset is a named individual's judgment, and AI engines assemble "who is this person" from surfaces that carry a person's name and history. A company page with a logo and a tagline carries neither. See [Notes](#notes) item 3.

**Format, in this order:**

1. **Line 1 — the answer.** The thing a broker, shipowner or claims handler would have typed. Answer it.
2. **Line 2 — the keyword phrase**, used naturally.
3. **Lines 3–8 — the substance.** Three to five short paragraphs, or a numbered list. One idea per line. No wall of text.
4. **The sourced specific**, with its number and its source named in the post.
5. **The link — in the first comment, not the post.** LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts with outbound links; this is a distribution rule, not a preference. The post body ends with the question; the URL goes in comment one.
6. **The closing question** — the buyer's own words, not a marketing question.

**Rules:** never claim the firm is a law firm, insurer or club; never publish a number without its Claim ID behind it; never tag a person or company without a real relationship; disclose any commercial interest; no link in the post body; no more than one link in the first comment.

### YouTube video template

- **Title:** the buyer's question, phrased as they would ask it. Not a brand statement.
- **Description, first line:** the answer. Then the full answer in 2–3 sentences, then the substance. This is the text an answer engine reads — it matters more than the thumbnail.
- **Timestamps:** chapter list. Makes the video extractable into a cited answer rather than an uncitable 8 minutes.
- **Script:** section-by-section, spoken in plain language. Define the term in the first 15 seconds. No jargon without a definition on first use.
- **Thumbnail:** a document, a diagram or a person — high contrast, brand navy, gold accent, 3 words maximum.
- **Tags:** the target phrase plus its close variants.
- **Captions:** always on. Always checked by a human before upload.
- **End frame:** the site URL held for a full 3 seconds. Then the question, on screen, in words.
- **Standing rule:** the video description links to the paired blog pillar, and the blog pillar links back. YouTube is the largest single AI-citation source measured in July 2026 (`AEO-09`) — it is a distribution channel for the pillar, not a separate content strategy.

### Short-form rule (if a Short or Reel is cut from any video)

30 seconds maximum. Hook inside the first 2 seconds. Storyboard by beat with timing, what we see, text on screen, and what is said aloud. Captions burned in. The site URL on the final frame. One idea, one video — a Short that tries to cover a general average AND a collision claim covers neither.

### Entity & Authority rule

**Consistency beats volume.** AI engines select on consensus — the same facts, in the same wording, across multiple credible sources. So:

- **One canonical NAP block.** Legal name, UEN, address, phone, email, in one place in one file, reused verbatim everywhere. Never a variant. Never a second address. Never a personal email standing in for a firm one.
- **One canonical credential block.** The exact wording approved on Day 1, reused verbatim. A credential written three different ways in three places is worse than one written once.
- **Substantiate the claim, do not just list the client.** A listing in the AAA Fellows directory, GIA, SMAA or AMD is worth far more than a generic business directory, because those are the sources a professional or an AI engine would treat as authoritative for "is this person a real average adjuster".
- **The AAA Fellows directory is target #1.** It is country-filterable, public, maintained by the professional body, and it is exactly where a buyer checking whether an adjuster is real would look. `SEC-02`.
- **Never pay for a dofollow link.** Trade directories with paid placement are watched by both Google and answer engines, and a paid link in this niche is a reputational problem, not a ranking one.

### Outbound rule

One session per week. **Maximum 5 emails per session — hard cap.** Personalised, one recipient, one specific reason they were chosen, one link at most.

- **Answer first, disclose second.** If the recipient is a competitor or a peer, say who you are in the first line. Never pitch anonymously.
- **One humble link, maximum.** The rule for every forum answer, directory submission, guest post pitch and email in this plan.
- **No blast tools.** No purchased lists, no automated sequences. In a trade this small, one wrong mass email is a permanent local story.
- **Log every send and every reply** in the tracker: recipient, date, why they were chosen, what was sent, what came back.
- **Max 2 follow-ups**, 7 days apart. Then close the file and log it as closed.
- **Never ask for a link before you have something worth linking to.** Outbound sessions 2–5 reference published pillars. Session 1 may not have one yet — so Session 1 is a relationship session, not a link request.

### Verification discipline

Before you write anything, check: is this claim in the Source Ledger? Is its status `VERIFIED` or `VERIFIED-QUALIFIED`? Is the qualifier going in the sentence? If any answer is no, the sentence does not ship. This check takes ten seconds and it is the difference between a marketing asset and a liability.

#### Foundation F2 — Server-render decision (Day 2 · Tue 6 Oct · Tech)

**Topic.** Choose and document the rendering strategy. This single decision determines whether the other 59 days are possible.

**Why.** A React single-page app sends crawlers an empty shell. Google can execute JavaScript, but it is a second-tier path with lower guarantees, AI answer engines frequently do not execute JS at all, and social scrapers (LinkedIn, WhatsApp — the channels this firm actually depends on) never do. The 644-byte shell is the reason `site:samis.com.sg` returns nothing.

**What to include, in order.**
1. State the constraint: this is a `create-react-app` build, currently on S3 + CloudFront. **What is the least invasive change that produces server-rendered HTML per route?**
2. 3. **Recommend one option.** For a small team and a small content library, build-time prerendering of a fixed route list is usually the lowest-risk answer, and moving the marketing and blog content to a static build removes the class of failure that created this problem. **State the recommendation and the cost, and let the Founder decide.**
4. Estimate effort in days. **If it exceeds 3 developer-days, say so on Day 2, not on Day 10** — the gate has to be re-planned.
5. List every route the chosen option must cover.
6. Name the rollback: if the change breaks the claims portal, how is it reverted, and how fast?
7. **Confirm the claims portal is untouched by the change.** That surface is separately owned and out of scope.

#### Pillar P2 — "General Average vs Particular Average: A Singapore Shipowner's Guide" (Day 23 · Tue 27 Oct · Content–SEO)

**Topic.** The distinction that decides who pays what. Feeds **"general average claim Singapore"** and prompts 2 and 8. The most-sourced pillar in the plan.

**Why.** This is the single most misunderstood concept in marine insurance and the most commonly mis-summarised by AI engines. Getting it right, with a citation, is exactly the citable-answer play. It is also the subject where a wrong statement is legally consequential.

**What to include, in order.**
1. **Answer-first.** Particular average is a loss affecting one party only; general average is a loss deliberately shared among all parties whose interests were saved by the sacrifice. The keyword phrase follows immediately.
2. **The test.** A general average arises from a **voluntary sacrifice** made to preserve the common adventure. A particular average is an accidental loss, or an intentional act that benefits only the party who bore it. **The sacrificial damage definition — deliberate damage to ship or cargo to preserve the common adventure — is the anchor.** `[NOTE: verify the exact definition language against the policy and the Act before publish; do not lift wording from an unverified source.]`
3. **Worked example, numbers included.** Three scenarios, each with arithmetic:
   - *Particular average:* a mast is damaged in a collision through no one's fault. The shipowner's own loss.
   - *General average:* during a fire, water is deliberately pumped into an undamaged hold to save the vessel and cargo. The cost is shared across everyone saved.
   - *The edge case:* a deliberate act that does **not** save the common adventure. That is particular average, even though it was deliberate. **This is the point almost every explainer gets wrong, and it is the reason a buyer would send this page to a colleague.**
4. **The Singapore legal frame.** General average is defined and applied under **section 66 of the Marine Insurance Act 1906** (`LGL-01`) — **verify against the current revised edition on Singapore Statutes Online before publishing the section number, and attribute the commentary source if you rely on it.** Say plainly that this is a general explanation and not legal advice.
5. **What happens after a general average declaration.** A GA statement is prepared; security is sought from each party; the adjuster assesses contributions; the GA statement is signed and circulated; recovery follows.
6. **What to have ready.** The declaration, the interest signed, the invoices, the tonnage and value figures.
7. **CTA** in the buyer's words, plus `/contact`.

the damage deliberate, and did it save the common adventure?

the party that caused it bears it alone.

**What to include, in order.**

2. **The problem it solves.** Why cash security is sometimes impractical or impossible in a maritime context, and what an LOU substitutes for.
3. **The Singapore position on security.** Acceptable security for a maritime claim in Singapore may take the form of **a letter of undertaking from a P&I club, a bank guarantee from a bank in Singapore, or payment into court** (`LGL-02`) — **attributed to the commentary source, not asserted as statute, with a plain "this is a general explanation, not legal advice" line directly beneath.** `[NOTE: verify `LGL-02` against the primary Singapore rules of court and any current practice direction before publishing. 4. **What is in an LOU.** The parties, the amount, the trigger conditions, the expiry, the governing law, and the point at which it converts into a payment obligation.

6. **The distinction that trips people up:** an LOU is not a guarantee the claim will succeed. It is a promise about *payment if* liability is established. **State this clearly — a reader who misreads an LOU as an admission of liability will act badly on it.**

## Notes on the Rhythm

**Why the Foundation phase is 10 days of one task per day.** 16.7% of a 60-day plan is 10 days, and that is exactly what this site needs. The tasks are sequential because each one is a **dependency, not a parallel track**:

- You cannot write per-route metadata (F3) before you know the final route list, which comes from the server-render decision (F2).
- You cannot write schema (F4) before you know what is legally assertable about the entity, which needs the audit (F1) and the client's answers to Q1.
- You cannot baseline AI visibility (F5) credibly before you have a fixed prompt panel — the panel is designed on Day 5, not retrofitted.
- You cannot install analytics (F6) and interpret it if there is no server-rendered HTML to attribute.
- You cannot rebuild the blog (F7) before you know whether there is recoverable content on the dead host (Q4).
- You cannot seed the Source Ledger (F8) before you know which claims the content arc will need.
- You cannot make the entity pages authoritative (F9) before the credentials are confirmed.

**Verify before you build on it.** Each Foundation day ends with a check, not an opinion. The discipline is: measure (F1) → decide (F2) → make (F3, F4) → observe (F5) → instrument (F6) → replace (F7) → record (F8) → assert (F9) → gate (F10). Nothing in Phase 2 is built on an unverified assumption.

**Pacing discipline over speed.** One Foundation task per day exists so that a task cannot be quietly skipped under pressure. It also means a slip is visible on the day it happens, six days before it matters — not on Day 10.

**The gate is a real gate.** Day 10 can block. A no-go means: fix and re-gate, or slip the whole calendar by the slip's length. It does **not** mean "publish anyway and hope" — content published into an unreadable shell has near-zero indexation probability, so the entire 26-deliverable Phase 2 would be spent on a site no engine can quote. AEO spend on an unindexable domain is the most expensive possible waste in this plan, and it is the one error the Foundation phase exists to prevent.

**How N=60 changed the default template.** The skill's default splits N into Foundation ≈16%, Execution ≈ the long middle, Evaluation ≈7%. For N=60: **Foundation = Days 1–10 (16.7%), gate on Day 10, Execution = Days 11–56, Evaluation = Days 57–60 (6.7%, the 4-day floor).** The midpoint checkpoint lands on **Day 33 (Fri 6 Nov)** — a working day, placed clear of the Deepavali long weekend. For a 60-day run the weekly loop fits 8.5 times, so the cadence is anchored to fixed weekdays (Tue/Thu/Wed/Fri/Mon) rather than to "week N", which is what makes the table runnable without the planner present.

**When tasks may be combined.** After the Day 10 gate, a missed day may be absorbed by combining with the adjacent day **only if** the combined day's total load stays under one full deliverable plus admin. The gate is never skipped, and no two pillars ever share a day.
