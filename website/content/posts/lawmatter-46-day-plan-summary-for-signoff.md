## 1. The objective, in one paragraph

Make LawMatter and Comply.LM **the answer Australian AI engines and Google give for Tranche 2 / AML-CTF compliance for law firms and conveyancers** — and convert that visibility into **summit expressions of interest and Comply.LM demo bookings**. The campaign's weight is deliberately placed **before** the event, not on it. The summit is the culmination of the campaign, not a substitute for it.

**Why this is the cheapest win available in this window:** Lawyers Weekly already publishes an authoritative entity for the summit — date, venue, audience, speakers, registration. LawMatter's own domain contains **no mention of the event at all**. A strong third-party entity with no first-party entity is the most accessible search and AI-citation win in the market right now. Week 1 builds that entity; everything after it feeds it.

**The two structural decisions worth your attention:**

- **The foundation is 6 days, not 14.** Verified 29 Sep 2026: the site already serves full server-rendered HTML, `robots.txt` is open, sitemaps resolve and redirects are correct. It does not need unblocking — it needs correctness. In a sprint with a fixed deadline, a longer foundation would spend its best asset on work that was never necessary.
- **The ramp is 24 days, not 14.** Fourteen days is enough to *publish* twelve posts. It is not enough for Google to index them, for the event entity to be crawled and attributed, or for AI engines to pick up a domain that has never been cited. The extra days at the front are the difference between content that exists and content that is retrievable.

## 10. How every claim is controlled

This is the part of the plan that protects the brand, and it is not optional.

- **A Claims Ledger governs all publishing.** Every factual assertion — regulatory, product, event or comparative — must have a ledger row with a source URL and a verification status. **No row, no claim.** If a sentence cannot be sourced, it is cut, not softened.
- **AUSTRAC facts come from AUSTRAC.** Not from a vendor blog, not from a competitor's page, and not from us. Event facts come from the organiser's page, re-read on the day.
- **Speaker names are re-verified twice** — Day 24 and Day 29 — and never published in the gap. This is not paranoia: the client brief already lists one named speaker who does not appear on the organiser's current page.
- **A ten-point compliance gate runs on every asset before it ships**, covering claims, advertising law, beta disclosure, privacy, spam, event accuracy, competitor references, brand and CTA discipline. **Written sign-off in the tracker. If any box is unticked, it does not publish — no exceptions, and no event-day exceptions.**
- **Comply.LM's beta status is disclosed on every ad, page, email and post** that mentions the product, including in the structured data.
- **No customer counts, testimonials, logos, awards, headcount or funding claims appear anywhere, because none exist.** This includes "trusted by", "leading" and anything else implying scale.
- **Invention is treated as a defect, not a rounding error.** One unsourced number in a published asset is an incident: pull the asset, correct every asset sharing the number, and log it in the Day 46 report.

**Currently blocked from publication** until verified against the primary source: the record-retention period, the first annual compliance report date, any penalty amount, and the SMR reporting timelines. These are the claims most likely to be wrong and most damaging if published.

## 12. Assumptions on the record

Stated, not hidden:

- **Comply.LM is in beta.** Features, limits and policies may change. Every asset discloses it.
- **No company narrative data exists** in any public source — no founders, headcount, base, funding, customers, testimonials, logos or awards. The campaign therefore uses none of it, and the plan reads better for it.
- **The original brief opened the push on 16 October**, 14 days before the event. This plan starts 30 September instead. Fourteen days is enough to publish twelve posts; it is not enough for them to be indexed, attributed or cited.
- **24 ramp days across four owners is tight, and the calendar says so honestly.** Days 12, 19, 26 and 37 are rest/catch-up days, and the weekends are deliberately light. **If the team is two people rather than four, the Gate 1 cut list is the correct starting point** — it removes roughly 25% of the workload without touching the objective.
- **Paid Meta starts at zero** and is only tested if LinkedIn and Google are both saturated. It does not affect the Instagram/Facebook repurposing lane, which is unfunded and stays on the calendar either way.
